Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to common pet health questions

When should I take my pet to the vet? +
You should take your pet to the vet if you notice sudden changes in behavior, loss of appetite, difficulty breathing, persistent vomiting or diarrhea, lethargy, unexplained weight loss, or any signs of pain. When in doubt, it's always better to consult with a veterinarian. Regular checkups at least once a year are also important for preventive care.
How can I tell if my pet is sick? +
Common signs of illness in pets include changes in eating or drinking habits, unusual lethargy, vomiting, diarrhea, coughing, difficulty breathing, changes in bathroom habits, excessive scratching or licking, and behavioral changes. Regular observation of your pet's normal behavior helps identify when something is wrong.
What are the most common pet health problems? +
Common pet health problems include dental disease, obesity, ear infections, skin allergies, arthritis, diabetes, kidney disease, urinary tract infections, and parasites like fleas and ticks. Regular vet checkups can help prevent and catch these issues early.
How often should I feed my pet? +
Most adult dogs do well with two meals per day, while puppies may need three to four smaller meals. Cats typically prefer smaller, more frequent meals throughout the day. The exact feeding schedule depends on your pet's age, breed, health status, and your veterinarian's recommendations.
What vaccinations does my pet need? +
Core vaccines for dogs include rabies, distemper, parvovirus, and adenovirus. Cats need rabies, feline distemper (panleukopenia), calicivirus, and rhinotracheitis vaccines. Non-core vaccines depend on lifestyle and location. Your veterinarian will create a vaccination schedule appropriate for your pet.
